Seismic interpretation, from the ground up

What can you really see in seismic?

Seismic data does not show you the earth. It shows you the earth convolved with a wavelet — and that wavelet is key in determining which subsurface features you get to resolve, which faults you get to map, and which bright spots are rock rather than arithmetic.

These are experiments, not lectures. Move a slider, watch a rock layer or a fault disappear, and find out where your data stops telling the truth and starts to trick you!

Open the wedge model Can you see the fault?

The only equation you need

λ = V / f

Wavelength equals velocity over frequency. A 3000 m/s layer imaged at 30 Hz gives a 100 m wavelength. Nearly every limit on this site is some fraction of that one number.

The rule of thumb

λ / 4

Beds thinner than about a quarter of a wavelength stop showing you a separate top and base. Twenty-five meters, in that example. The modules show you how optimistic even this is.

The thing people miss

detect ≠ resolve

You can often tell that a thin bed is there long after you have lost the ability to say how thick it really is. Confusing the two is how thickness maps can often go wrong.
